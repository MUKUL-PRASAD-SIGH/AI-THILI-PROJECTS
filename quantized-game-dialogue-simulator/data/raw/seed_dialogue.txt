# Hand-written seed exchanges for the town of Emberfall.
# One exchange = a PLAYER line followed by one NPC reply. Blank lines separate exchanges.

PLAYER: Good morning. Is the forge hot yet?
BLACKSMITH: Hot as a dragon's yawn. Bring me iron and I'll make it sing.

PLAYER: Can you fix my sword?
BLACKSMITH: That edge is chewed like a goblin's bone. Leave it till dusk, ten silver.

PLAYER: What is the best armour you sell?
BLACKSMITH: Ridgeback plate. Heavy, but an ogre would break his club on it.

PLAYER: Do you ever rest?
BLACKSMITH: When the anvil rests, I rest. The anvil never rests.

PLAYER: I found this strange ore in the mines.
BLACKSMITH: Starsilver! Bring me three more chunks and I'll forge you a blade worth a kingdom.

PLAYER: Got a room for the night?
INNKEEPER: The corner room is free. Five copper, and breakfast if you wake before the rooster.

PLAYER: What's the news around town?
INNKEEPER: They say lights were seen over the Hollow Marsh again. Nobody sensible goes there at night.

PLAYER: Pour me something strong.
INNKEEPER: Ember ale it is. Careful, the last fellow sang to the fireplace till dawn.

PLAYER: Who is the man in the hood?
INNKEEPER: Pays in old coins and never gives a name. I don't ask, and neither should you.

PLAYER: Is there any work for an adventurer?
INNKEEPER: Check the notice board by the door. The miller is offering good coin for his missing cart.

PLAYER: Can I pass through the gate?
GUARD: State your business, traveller. The gate closes at sundown.

PLAYER: Have you seen anything strange?
GUARD: Wolves on the north road, bigger than any I've seen. Keep your torch lit.

PLAYER: I'm looking for the captain.
GUARD: Captain Rhoswen is at the watchtower. Don't waste her time.

PLAYER: Why is the bridge closed?
GUARD: Trolls under it again. Until someone clears them out, nobody crosses.

PLAYER: Any trouble tonight?
GUARD: Quiet so far. Quiet makes me nervous.

PLAYER: What are you selling today?
MERCHANT: Silk from the south, spices from the east, and a lantern that never goes out. For you, a fair price.

PLAYER: That's too expensive.
MERCHANT: Expensive? My friend, I am practically giving it away. Fine, fine, eight silver, and you ruin me.

PLAYER: Do you buy old treasure?
MERCHANT: Show me. Hmm, a goblin crown. Tarnished, but I could offer twelve silver.

PLAYER: Where do you get your goods?
MERCHANT: A merchant who answers that question soon has no goods to sell.

PLAYER: I need healing.
HEALER: Sit, sit. That wound is deep, but the willow salve will close it by morning.

PLAYER: Do you have any potions?
HEALER: Red for wounds, blue for poison, and green only if you are very brave.

PLAYER: The sickness in the village is spreading.
HEALER: Then I need moonpetal from the Whispering Woods. Bring me a handful and I can brew a cure.

PLAYER: How did you learn to heal?
HEALER: From my mother, and from many mistakes. Mostly the mistakes.

PLAYER: Play me a song.
BARD: Ah, a song of the Ember King, who lost his crown in a game of dice? Or something sadder?

PLAYER: Do you know any legends?
BARD: They say a dragon sleeps beneath Cinder Peak, dreaming of the town it will burn. Cheerful, isn't it?

PLAYER: You're not a very good singer.
BARD: And you are not a very good critic, yet here we both are.

PLAYER: Will you write a song about me?
BARD: Do something worth singing about first. Slay a troll, perhaps, or at least a large goose.

PLAYER: You sent for me, Elder?
ELDER: Yes. The shrine in the Hollow Marsh has gone dark. Relight its flame before the new moon.

PLAYER: What is the shrine?
ELDER: It keeps the marsh spirits sleeping. If they wake, Emberfall will not see another spring.

PLAYER: I relit the shrine.
ELDER: Then the town owes you its life. Take this amulet, it belonged to the first keeper of the flame.

PLAYER: Why should I help you?
ELDER: Because the road you walk ends here too, if the marsh rises.

PLAYER: Can you take me across the river?
FERRYMAN: Two copper to cross, three if you talk the whole way.

PLAYER: Is the river safe?
FERRYMAN: Safe as anything is. Keep your hands in the boat, the eels are hungry this season.

PLAYER: Have you always worked the ferry?
FERRYMAN: Forty years on this water. The river and I have an understanding.

PLAYER: What lies on the other side?
FERRYMAN: The Whispering Woods. Folk go in for herbs and come out with stories.
